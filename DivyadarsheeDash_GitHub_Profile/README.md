## 🔬 Research Identity

I am an **AI/ML student and aspiring researcher** working on a central question:

> **How can intelligent models remain accurate, explainable, and dependable when they move beyond notebooks and operate inside real physical systems?**

My interests lie at the intersection of **machine learning, deep learning, physics-guided intelligence, digital twins, fault diagnosis, computer vision, power electronics, and control systems**.

I am particularly interested in combining **physical knowledge with data-driven learning** instead of treating AI models as black boxes. My goal is not to produce impressive-looking training scores. I want to build systems that remain useful under **noise, limited sensors, class imbalance, changing operating conditions, and strict evaluation**.

## 🧠 Research Principles

```text
Real data          > polished toy demonstrations
Honest validation  > inflated performance claims
Physical insight   + machine learning
Reproducibility    + clear engineering reasoning
Research depth     > unnecessary complexity
```

I aim to develop each project as a **defensible research contribution** supported by:

- a clearly defined problem and threat or operating model,
- meaningful baselines,
- leakage-free evaluation,
- ablation studies,
- uncertainty and failure analysis,
- reproducible experiments,
- and conclusions supported by evidence.
